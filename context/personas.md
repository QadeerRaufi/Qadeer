# Research and personas: Rya Rightsizing

## What the client sells, where, to whom
- Rya Rightsizing offers compassionate rightsizing, decluttering and move support for elders, led by founder Lisa Fisher, with a family liaison as single point of contact. Process: Assess, Plan, Sort, Move, Settle.
- Serves North & West Vancouver and the Sea to Sky corridor, with an office in Squamish. The plan focuses first on existing Squamish and Sea to Sky relationships, with selective expansion to the North Shore depending on Lisa's capacity.
- Serves adult children (often the buyer, often far away) and elders (the person at the centre), plus referral partners such as retirement residences, realtors and community organizations. Two of the first four clients came through referrals; contact often starts during illness, memory change or another urgent trigger.

## Buyer vs. user
| Role | Who | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| Decides and pays | Usually the adult child; sometimes the elder | Trust, clear communication, knowing it is handled |
| Receives the service | The elder | Dignity, own pace, keeping what matters |
| Influences the decision | Residence move-in coordinators and community-relations staff, realtors, Better at Home and similar organizations | Their client or resident having a smooth, reliable transition |

## Market snapshot
- **Category:** Senior move support and rightsizing, which a family would likely call "help moving my parent." `(assumption – validate with client)`
- **Alternatives a buyer considers:**
  - Do nothing and wait for a crisis
  - Family does it themselves (the adult child travels in, or siblings split it)
  - A general moving company or junk removal service (the brand book positions Rya against these)
  - Help from the residence or realtor
  - Other move managers or senior transition services `(assumption – none named in sources)`
- **Trigger moments:** illness, memory loss or an unexpected change in living situation (plan); a move to a retirement residence; a home sale; an estate after a death (brand book).

## Persona 1: The Long-Distance Adult Child (primary buyer)
- **Who they are:** An adult son or daughter, busy with their own work and family, who lives far from a parent. Often becomes the point person for a move.
- **Situation right now:** A parent needs to move or the house needs clearing, and the adult child cannot be there day to day. They are working out who can do it.
- **Goal:** A trustworthy local person who handles logistics and keeps them informed, so they can support their parent without taking over.
- **Fears:**
  - Practical: not knowing where to start, hiring the wrong people, a rushed or botched move, things going missing.
  - Emotional: guilt about not being there; worry that their parent is overwhelmed.
  - Social: conflict with siblings; their parent feeling their choices were taken away.
- **What reassures them:** One point of contact; clear updates; a complimentary consultation (in-home or virtual) with no pressure; proof that decisions stay with their parent.
- **Where they look for help:** Referrals from residences, realtors or Better at Home; online search; Facebook (plan). `(assumption – validate with client)` for search habits beyond the plan.
- **In their words** `(assumption – illustrative, not real quotes)`: "I live too far away to be there every week." / "I don't know where to even start with Mom's house." / "I don't want to take over, but I can't do this alone."
- **Message cue:** "You can stay close to your parent while someone local handles the details."

## Persona 2: The Elder Facing a Move (person at the centre)
- **Who they are:** An elder staying in place, moving somewhere smaller, or heading to a retirement residence. Some contact Rya themselves; many are brought in by family.
- **Situation right now:** Sorting a lifetime of belongings and making decisions feels like too much, and they may not know where to start. Some are recovering from illness or a change in health (plan).
- **Goal:** Keep what matters, make decisions at their own pace and feel respected throughout.
- **Fears:**
  - Practical: not being able to manage the physical work or the logistics.
  - Emotional: losing things tied to memories; grief about leaving a home.
  - Social: being treated as a burden or as someone who can't decide for themselves.
- **What reassures them:** "You decide what stays." Patient, unhurried support, shown with capable, relaxed elders in imagery, not frailty.
- **Where they look for help:** Senior centres and workshops (Lisa already delivers these), residences, family. `(assumption – validate with client)` for online habits.
- **In their words** `(assumption – illustrative, not real quotes)`: "I've got a lifetime of things here and I don't know where to begin." / "I'm not ready to let everything go." / "I just want to do this my way."
- **Message cue:** "Keep what matters. We help with the rest, at your pace."

## Persona 3: The Referral Partner (residence staff or realtor)
- **Who they are:** A move-in coordinator, community-relations staff member or seniors-focused realtor who regularly sees families struggle with a move. The plan names these as the primary target audience.
- **Situation right now:** They meet families at a stressful moment and often get asked "who can help with the move?" They are cautious about recommending someone unproven.
- **Goal:** A dependable, professional person they can hand families to, which makes their own job smoother.
- **Fears:**
  - Practical: a referral who is unreliable or slow to respond.
  - Emotional: a family left unhappy with someone they recommended.
  - Social: their reputation suffering if the referral goes badly. `(assumption – validate with client)`
- **What reassures them:** A short, clear description of what Rya does and when to refer; a useful guide they can hand over; Lisa as a familiar, steady presence.
- **Where they look for help:** In-person visits, email follow-up, professional networks (plan). Residences may restrict outside visitors or marketing, so permission comes first (plan).
- **In their words** `(assumption – illustrative, not real quotes)`: "Families ask me who can help with the move." / "I need someone I can trust to show up." / "I can't recommend someone I haven't met."
- **Message cue:** "A reliable, kind local contact for the families you already support."
