# Rya Rightsizing — Voice and Tone

## 1. Brand personality

Rya Rightsizing communicates like a calm, capable friend who understands that moving later in life is both a practical and emotional transition.

**Warm, not overly sentimental**
Show genuine care without exaggerated emotion or overly affectionate language.

**Capable, not clinical**
Communicate practical knowledge and organization without sounding cold or overly technical.

**Respectful, not condescending**
Treat seniors as capable individuals who make their own decisions. Never speak down to them.

**Reassuring, not alarmist**
Offer practical support and confidence without creating fear or urgency.

**Hopeful, not falsely cheerful**
Recognize that transitions can be difficult while focusing on meaningful possibilities for the next chapter.

## 2. Tone by situation

### First contact
Warm, welcoming and informative. Explain how Rya can help without creating pressure.

Example: "Every move is different. We can talk through what matters to you and how we might help."

### Educational content
Clear, practical and encouraging. Give readers genuinely useful advice they can apply independently.

Example: "Start with the belongings you use most often. This can help you understand what matters in your next home."

### Follow-up communication
Personal, concise and respectful. Offer the next step without aggressive sales language.

Example: "Thank you for taking the time to speak with us. We're here if you have any questions about the next steps."

### Sensitive moments
Quiet, patient and compassionate. Acknowledge grief or difficult circumstances without assuming how someone feels.

Example: "Sorting through a loved one's belongings can take time. We'll work at a pace that feels right for your family."

### Professional referral outreach
Friendly, knowledgeable and collaborative. Focus on developing trust rather than pushing for immediate referrals.

Example: "We support older adults and their families through rightsizing and moving. We'd welcome the opportunity to learn more about the families you work with."

## 3. Words and phrases to use

- Rightsizing
- Downsizing (secondary term)
- Next chapter
- At your pace
- Making space for what matters
- Dignity
- Peace of mind
- Meaningful
- Compassionate
- Support
- Guidance
- Belongings
- Older adults
- Complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual
- Three-hour blocks of time or more

## 4. Words and phrases to avoid

- Elderly
- Old folks
- Hoarder (as a label)
- Purge
- Get rid of
- Junk (when referring to meaningful belongings)
- Cluttered mess
- Cheap
- Fast or quick (as service promises)
- Before it's too late
- Free consultation
- Three-hour minimum
- Sessions (for service bookings)
- No long-term contract
- All-or-nothing
- Metro Vancouver

Avoid language that creates guilt, fear or unnecessary pressure.

## 5. Writing conventions

- Use "we" when speaking for Rya and "you" when addressing the reader.
- Use Canadian spelling.
- Write short-to-medium sentences.
- Aim for a Grade 7–8 reading level.
- Use clear language instead of marketing jargon.
- Use exclamation marks rarely.
- Avoid emojis in service-related copy.
- Keep social media content friendly but professional.
- Lead with "rightsizing" and use "downsizing" as a supporting search term.

## 6. Before-and-after examples

### Example 1 — Service promotion

**Before:**
"Get rid of unwanted clutter fast with our affordable senior moving services!"

**After:**
"Rightsizing is about making space for what matters. We help older adults sort through belongings and prepare for their next chapter at a comfortable pace."

**Why:** Removes urgency and dismissive language while emphasizing dignity and practical support.

### Example 2 — Consultation invitation

**Before:**
"Book your FREE consultation today before moving becomes overwhelming!"

**After:**
"Not sure where to begin? Book a complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual, and we'll talk through the next steps together."

**Why:** Replaces fear-based selling with a calm, welcoming invitation.

## 7. Brand consistency checklist

Before approving any content, confirm:

- Does the message sound warm and capable?
- Does it respect the independence and dignity of older adults?
- Is the language clear and practical?
- Does it avoid fear, guilt and urgency?
- Does it use approved service terminology?
- Does it reflect Rya's emphasis on genuine relationships and trust?
